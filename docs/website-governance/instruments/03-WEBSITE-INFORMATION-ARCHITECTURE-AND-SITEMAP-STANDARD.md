**Status:** Ready for Founder Ratification  
**Authority:** CLN-WEB-CON-001; CLN-WEB-POL-002  
**Owner:** Website Business Owner  
**Canonical Rule Ownership:** Navigation, page families, canonical sitemap, URLs, and page creation or retirement  
**Effective Date:** Upon ratification  
**Review Cycle:** Semiannual and before a material structural change  
**Supersedes:** CLN-WEB-STD-003 v1.1.0  

---

## 1. Purpose

This Standard establishes the canonical website structure.

The site shall be commercially focused, easy to navigate, free of duplicate page programs, and designed around a clear assessment path.

---

## 2. Architectural Principles

The website shall:

- Organize content around visitor needs.
- Keep important content within a shallow navigation structure.
- Give each page one primary purpose.
- Use one canonical page for each subject.
- Distinguish service-method content from industry-context content.
- Follow the territory rule in CLN-WEB-POL-004.
- Follow the conversion rule in CLN-WEB-POL-007.

A keyword is not sufficient justification for a page.

---

## 5. Home Page Requirements

The Home page must answer:

- What does Cleanital do?
- Who does Cleanital serve?
- Where does Cleanital operate?
- Why is Cleanital credible and different?
- What should the visitor do next?

The Home page shall not reproduce every service or industry page.

Geographic qualification shall follow CLN-WEB-POL-004 §4.

---

## 6. Service and Industry Distinction

A service page and a related industry page may both exist only when they perform different jobs.

### Service Page

A service page explains:

- The service definition.
- Scope and exclusions.
- Methods and process.
- Assessment inputs.
- Quality approach.
- Related services.
- Appropriate use cases.

### Industry Page

An industry page explains:

- Facility-specific operating conditions.
- Common risks and needs.
- Decision-maker concerns.
- Relevant combinations of services.
- Client responsibilities.
- Industry-specific assessment questions.

A service page shall not become an industry page with a different heading. An industry page shall not repeat a service page with industry keywords inserted.

Before publication, paired pages must pass a distinct-value review. If at least one-third of the substantive sections would be duplicated, the pages shall be consolidated or rewritten.

---

## 7. Page-Family Rules

### Services

One canonical page per approved service. Related pages may cross-link but shall not compete for the same purpose.

### Industries

An additional industry page requires evidence of service readiness, distinct visitor value, an owner, and supportable claims.

### Resources

Resources must educate or answer recurring questions. They shall not be used to create hidden service or location page clusters.

### Portals

Portal pages are routing pages only and must pass the activation gate in CLN-WEB-POL-002.

### Service Areas

The Service Areas page implements CLN-WEB-POL-004. No child service-area pages are permitted.

---

## 8. URL Rules

URLs shall:

- Be lowercase.
- Use hyphens.
- Avoid unnecessary dates and parameters.
- Avoid city, county, metro, and “near me” variants.
- Avoid internal or private identifiers.
- Remain stable after publication where practical.
- Use redirects when a published URL changes.
- Reflect hierarchy where a true parent-child relationship exists.

---

## 9. Page Creation Record

A routine page creation requires ten items:

1. Title and URL.
2. Page family.
3. Purpose.
4. Intended audience.
5. Owner.
6. Primary action.
7. Claims or evidence needed.
8. Related canonical pages.
9. Approval.
10. Review date.

Additional privacy, security, legal, integration, or accessibility fields are required only when the page creates those impacts.

---

## 10. Retirement

A page shall be retired or consolidated when it is duplicative, stale, unsupported, ownerless, misleading, inconsistent with current services, or no longer useful.

Retirement requires:

- Redirect, removal, or archive decision.
- Internal-link update.
- Sitemap update.
- Search-index treatment.
- Lightweight or material change record as applicable.

---

## 11. Prohibited Patterns

Prohibited patterns include:

- City, county, metro, neighborhood, and “near me” service-area pages.
- Multiple pages for one service with minor wording changes.
- Doorway pages.
- Hidden navigation pages.
- False local-office pages.
- Public copies of secure portal screens.
- Ownerless campaign pages.
- Automated page proliferation.
- Service/industry twins that fail the distinct-value test.

---

## 12. Material Structural Changes

The following are material:

- Adding or removing a top-level navigation item.
- Adding or removing a page family.
- Changing the primary conversion destination.
- Changing service-area architecture.
- Creating a new form class.
- Converting a routing page into a workflow.
- Changing a published canonical URL without a redirect plan.
- Materially changing the service/industry taxonomy.

Routine link, wording, and ordering changes may use the lightweight change log when they do not change meaning or user commitment.

---

## 13. Conformance Evidence

Maintain:

- Current sitemap.
- Canonical URL list.
- Redirects.
- Orphan-page and broken-link results.
- Duplicate-content review.
- Active portal list.
- Page owners and review dates.
