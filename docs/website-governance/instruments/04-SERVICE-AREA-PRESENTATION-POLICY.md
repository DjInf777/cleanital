---

## 1. Purpose

This Policy is the single normative home for Cleanital’s public service-area rule.

Other governance instruments may cross-reference this Policy but shall not create alternate geographic wording requirements.

---

## 2. Approved Public Service Area

Cleanital’s public service area is presented only as:

- North Carolina.
- South Carolina.

Actual serviceability is determined through Cleanital’s qualification process.

---

## 3. Core Qualification

Each page or self-contained public context that makes a service-area claim must communicate that availability depends on qualification.

The qualification does not have to appear in every sentence.

A short headline, navigation label, metadata title, or brand statement may say “North Carolina and South Carolina” without repeating the qualification when:

- The same page or context contains the qualifying statement in a reasonably discoverable location; and
- The short statement does not independently promise universal availability.

A standalone advertisement, embedded card, social preview, or other context that may be seen without the page qualification must include its own limitation where omission could imply guaranteed service.

Approved qualification concept:

> Service availability depends on facility location, scope, scheduling requirements, and current operational capacity.

Equivalent wording is permitted when it preserves the meaning.

---

## 4. Approved Brand and Metadata Use

The following kinds of short statements are permitted:

- Commercial Cleaning in North Carolina and South Carolina.
- Serving organizations across North Carolina and South Carolina.
- Janitorial and facility-cleaning services in North Carolina and South Carolina.

The page or self-contained context must still satisfy §3.

---

## 5. Prohibited Geographic Content

The website shall not:

- Create city-, county-, metro-, neighborhood-, or “near me” service-area pages.
- Publish city lists as a substitute for qualification.
- Create hidden geographic sections for search indexing.
- Duplicate service pages by location.
- Claim a local office where none exists.
- Imply guaranteed statewide availability.
- Use a map that implies universal coverage.
- Publish false local-business structured data.
- Allow advertising or metadata to contradict this Policy.

---

## 6. Permitted Local References

A city, county, facility, or local reference may appear when factually necessary in:

- A case study.
- An approved testimonial.
- A legal or contact address.
- A job posting.
- A contractor opportunity.
- A specific operational notice.
- A company-history statement.

A local reference does not create a service-area page or general availability claim.

---

## 7. Forms

Commercial inquiry forms may ask for state, address, or postal code when needed for qualification.

Forms shall not:

- Display a public “served cities” list.
- Automatically confirm or reject service based solely on geography.
- Treat selection of North Carolina or South Carolina as a service guarantee.

An “Other” state option may capture out-of-area interest only when the form clearly preserves the approved public territory.

---

## 8. Maps and Structured Data

A map is optional.

If used, it must:

- Represent only the two-state public territory.
- Include or be paired with the qualification required by §3.
- Avoid invented office locations.
- Avoid city landing-page links.
- Remain understandable without color alone.

Structured data shall reflect actual offices and approved service-area facts.

---

## 9. Campaigns

Local advertising targeting does not authorize a permanent local landing-page program.

A temporary campaign page must:

- Remain within an approved page family.
- Avoid implying a local office or guaranteed capacity.
- Have an owner and removal date.
- Comply with the qualification rule.

---

## 10. Change Control

Changing either approved state, the state-level presentation model, or the prohibition on city-level pages requires:

- Constitutional amendment.
- Operational-capacity analysis.
- Exact old and new rule.
- Content, form, metadata, advertising, and structured-data impact review.
- Effective date and rollback plan.

Routine wording adjustments that preserve §§2–5 may use the lightweight change log.

---

## 11. Enforcement Evidence

Review:

- Indexed pages.
- Titles and descriptions.
- Structured data.
- Maps.
- Forms.
- Campaign pages.
- Service and industry pages.
- Footer and navigation.

Any conflicting geographic content shall be removed or corrected.
